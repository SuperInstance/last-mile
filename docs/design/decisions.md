| D42 | 2026-10-28 | **Presence mode defaults from PoC A:** conditioned lanes beat point mode at equal compute (presence 5 Hz + lane: 88% acc / 3 spurious flips per agent-min at 300 calls vs point: 83% / 34 flips, and point needs 5× the server batches). | D55 | 2026-10-28 | **Thinking time is gameplay** (Casey: "that time delay is the game"). Each thinking tier has a price in ticks (reflex 0; d1-3B ≈ 5; Jev ≈ 7; tev1 ≈ 40; cloud System-2 ≈ 100s) and a quality. A thought lands at `asked + measured latency + lead`, applied to a world that has moved on. Agents choose the tier per request (policy: urgent → fast tier, planning → slow tier; budgets price both money and time). The piano roll shows each thought as a bar from ask to landing, and the HUD shows tier choices. Latency estimates per tier come from the RTT estimator, so the game adapts to the real network and GPUs | It turns the never-wait design (D11) into a strategic choice instead of a hidden cost: think fast or think well | E4/E4c latency tables; [phase 3 plan](../superpowers/plans/2026-10-28-m1-phase3-agents.md) |

| D56 | 2026-10-28 | **Agents manipulate their own time-to-cognition ratio** (Casey). The levers are the agent's, set in its harness (`policy.toml`, `gates.toml`) and learnable between rounds: (1) **buy time**: hold or follow the safe branch (D45) while a slow tier thinks; (2) **think ahead**: pre-think likely situations in quiet moments (D31, extended to plans); (3) **ask less**: smaller question packs and tighter facts; (4) **reuse**: the verdict cache (D53); (5) **promote**: repeated good thoughts become reflexes (D25); (6) **delegate**: the leader thinks slowly, members act fast; (7) **pay**: spend budget to jump the queue. New metric beside the model-vs-code dial (D40): ticks of thinking per decision vs the decision quality it bought, per agent and per squad | Extends D55: the delay is the game, and steering it is the skill. Phase 3 Task 5 exposes levers 1–4 and 7; phase 5 measures the ratio and lets the reviewer propose ratio changes; lever 5 is M2 | D55 |

| D57 | 2026-10-28 | **Cognition is priced on three axes: time, money and model size** (Casey: "the money-to-cognition ratio… an additional budget on their cloud use or model-size constraints"). Each tier in the `TierTable` (D55) carries latency (ticks), money (tokens × the exchange rate, D16) and a size class (≤1B, ≤3B, ≤9B, cloud). The **round header sets the economy**: per-role cloud allotments, an optional bonus cloud budget, and per-role size caps (for example members ≤3B, leaders up to cloud). This yields game variants (a poor squad, small minds, a rich leader) that are fair, visible and replayable. Agents optimise quality per tick and quality per dollar inside the caps. New metrics: quality per token and quality per tick, per agent and per squad | Extends D55/D56 from time to money and size; turns budgets from limits into a strategic economy | D16, D55, D56 |

| D58 | 2026-10-28 | **The round's clock is a parameter:** a T-minus time countdown (`time_limit_ticks`), a **token-limit countdown** (the round ends when a squad's or the shared thinking budget is spent; `rules.end_on = time | tokens | both`), or both. The HUD shows whichever clock is running, and agents see their remaining clock in their facts, so strategy shifts with the clock (spend early vs hoard) | Casey: "sometimes it's on a time-minus countdown, other times a token-limit countdown, like my subscriptions" | D16, D55–D57 |

| D59 | 2026-10-29 | **Find hard, check cheap: certificate-carrying decisions.** Expensive minds search; every node verifies. Decisions may carry a short, code-checkable certificate (first ones: the safe branch's known-free cells and the facts key the decision was made on), checked before apply; a failed certificate is a refusal | The decision log is already the certificate of a round and replay its checker (like Lean for proofs); making it per-decision keeps verification cheap however costly the thought. Prompted by the ETH video and Casey's "we bypass the hard parts intuitively" | [fleet/09](../research/fleet/09-find-hard-check-cheap.md) |

| D60 | 2026-10-29 | **First learning target, found by testing from the outside:** after the fairness fix, standing still on a beacon out-scores the default reflex on some seeds (seed 3: 1611 vs 861 when squad 0 freezes at tick 1500), and 67% of 200 rounds end in a draw at the 9000-tick limit. Phase 5's first sealed prediction should target this (a "hold what you have" reflex or block), and phase 5 balancing should tune win_score and time_limit for decisiveness | `lm branch` turned a pinned test into a gameplay discovery; this is exactly the loop the game is about | fairness merge 5595db9 |

| D61 | 2026-10-29 | **Default beacons: 4 (was 3); the count must be even.** Beacons are laid out in 180°-mirrored pairs; on an even-sized grid the rotation centre falls between cells, so a single "centre" beacon can't be point-symmetric and favours one squad. `Params::validate` refuses an odd count | The fairness investigation (5595db9): asymmetric beacon placement was a root cause of squad 1 winning 101-52 | fairness merge |
| D62 | 2026-10-29 | **The default "fast" System One tier is d1-2B, not d1-3B; action confidence gate 0.60.** Over 200 fresh paired scenes, d1-2B was right on `action` 96% of the time vs 84% for d1-3B (26 vs 2 discordant pairs), kept 91% coverage at 1.00 accuracy at conf >= 0.70 (3B kept 49%), and is faster (132 vs 156 ms median). d1-omni-600M's confidence is uncalibrated (0.45 accuracy even at conf >= 0.90), so it stays off decision questions, and a 600M-first cascade is rejected. | D63 | 2026-10-29 | **Draws are mirror ties, not timeouts; the scoring knobs don't fix them (corrects D60).** Reflex vs reflex on the point-symmetric map: 63% draws for every win_score in 900..1800 and time limit in 9000..12000, and all of them end before the limit with both squads reaching win_score on the same tick. Decisiveness has to come from breaking the mirror. Options for Casey: (a) leave it, since thinking agents (model tiers, talk, learning) break the symmetry, and a reflex mirror match *should* draw; (b) seeded per-squad variation in the reflex; (c) a tie-break rule (e.g. most beacon-hold ticks, or first to reach win_score earlier in the tick order). Not decided | The draw is information: it says the reflexes are equal. Thinking is what should win (D55) | lm-core/tests/decisiveness.md |

| D70 | 2026-10-29 | **Split the agent's question pack: the move pack on every think, the talk pack only when there is something to say.** On the Thor's d1 server a thought costs about 11 ms per option, one after another (R2 0.994). | D71 | 2026-10-29 | **Escalate unsure answers to clef-flash.** d1-2B answers; when its confidence on a decision is < 0.5, clef-flash (:8096) is asked and its answer is taken.
