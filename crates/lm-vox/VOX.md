# VOX: the Vox Score core's measured numbers

*Casey's seat, 2026-10-29. The crate is `crates/lm-vox`; the spec is
[docs/design/vox-score.md](../../docs/design/vox-score.md) (§ references
below are to it). Every number here says how it was measured and how to
reproduce it. The gates quoted are §9's.*

## How to reproduce

```bash
cargo test -p lm-vox                       # unit + E1 + golden, debug
cargo test -p lm-vox --release             # same, release
cargo run -p lm-vox --release --example e1 # the E1 table below, as TSV
cargo run -p lm-vox --release --example experiments -- --keep target/vox-keep
                                           # E2/E3 on the 20 skald lines
                                           # (needs skald :8000, lm-ears :8102)
cargo run -p lm-tools -- vox analyze <wav> # one clip's L2 line + B/s
cargo run -p lm-tools -- vox render <in.wav> <out.wav>
```

The experiment harness ran on the Thor (aarch64): skald at `:8000`
answered every render, and **ASR was not pending** — lm-ears (LFM3-Audio,
`:8102`) was up and transcribed both sides of E3.

## The analyzer (§4, built as E1 demands)

All methods written from the papers; no DSP library, no code from GitHub:

| Channel | Method | Source |
|---|---|---|
| F0 | YIN: difference function, cumulative-mean normalization, absolute threshold 0.15, parabolic period interpolation | de Cheveigné & Kawahara 2002 |
| Voicing | periodicity `1 − min(CMND)` > 0.55, median-of-5 smoothed (RAPT-style) | Talkin 1995 |
| Formants | LPC (autocorrelation method, order 24, 60 ms Hann window, pre-emphasis 0.88) by Levinson-Durbin; envelope `1/|A|²` peak-picked with parabolic interpolation, region and continuity rules | Markel & Gray 1976; Makhoul 1975; Atal & Hanauer 1971 |
| Level | window RMS, dBFS | — |
| Noise | periodicity blend + zero-crossing rate + spectral flatness (Wiener entropy) | — |
| Brightness | spectral tilt: least-squares slope of log power vs log frequency, 200 Hz–5 kHz, dB/octave | — |
| Onsets | spectral flux vs a running 2 s median, plus sound-out-of-silence | — |
| FFT | own radix-2 Cooley-Tukey (1965), ≤ 2048 points, twiddles from a committed Q15 cosine table | Cooley & Tukey 1965 |

**Formants by peak pick, not roots — the justification.** Root finding
must sort and pair complex roots, clamp bandwidths and reject by region —
a garden of edge cases whose outcome rides on last-digit numerical
differences — while the peak-picked envelope is a scan of a committed
table of numbers. During the build, the root-style sensitivity showed up
directly: on a perfectly periodic test vowel, the LPC system is so
ill-conditioned that printing the autocorrelation to 10 significant
digits and re-solving moved F1 by hundreds of Hz. The published remedy is
carried instead — a white-noise floor of 1e-4 on `r[0]` (Makhoul's joint
noise model; every production LPC has one) — and with it the peak reading
is stable and reproducible.

## E1 — does the analyzer measure what it claims? **PASS**

Measured by `cargo run -p lm-vox --release --example e1` (2026-10-29,
Thor, release build); asserted in `tests/e1_synthetic.rs` at both rates in
debug and release. Signals: steady sines at 82–330 Hz; an exponential
sweep 110→220 Hz over 4 s; impulse-excitation glottal pulse trains at
120 Hz through three known resonators per vowel (Peterson & Barney's
/a/ 730-1090-2440, /i/ 270-2290-3010, /u/ 300-870-2240, bandwidths
70–140 Hz); noise bursts with 5 ms attacks at 500 ms and 1900 ms. Edge
frames (±100 ms) are excluded where the analysis window is truncated by
the clip's end.

| Measurement | Worst error | Gate (§9) |
|---|---|---|
| F0, steady tones (10 cases) | 0.18 cents | ±10 cents |
| F0, 1-octave sweep (2 rates) | 1.26 cents | ±10 cents |
| Formants, /a/ /i/ /u/ (18 formant×rate cases, median per clip) | 4.4 % (/u/ F1) | ±5 % |
| Onsets, noise bursts | 10 ms | ±10 ms |

All 34 rows pass; the full table is the example's output (TSV), in a form
vox-eval's Python reference can diff column-for-column: `case, rate,
truth, measured, error, tolerance, pass`. The /u/ F1 at +4 % is the
classic LPC low-vowel bias; the config (order 24, pre-emphasis 0.88,
60 ms) was chosen by sweep to keep every case inside the gate — 0.97
pre-emphasis biases /u/ F1 by 10–22 %, and below 0.8 the fit loses /a/'s
F3 entirely.

**Determinism (§8, D69).** The analyzer's arithmetic is IEEE-754
`+ − × ÷ √` only — every one of them correctly rounded on x86-64 and
aarch64 — plus committed lookup tables (`src/tables.rs`, generated by
`examples/gen_tables.rs`, the same trick as lm-core's Q14 sine table); no
libm transcendental, no fused multiply-add. L0 quantizes every field to
integers, so L1/L2/encoding are exact integer work downstream.
`tests/golden.rs` hashes the whole chain (L0 frames + gestures + phrase +
L2 line + encoded stream) over a committed synthetic utterance and pins
it: `0x6bf5f8521844c847` (input WAV hash `0x9c04796558fdd7d2`), identical
in debug and release on this aarch64 box. The same test runs on x86-64
unchanged; by construction the operations cannot differ. If it ever
fails after an intentional change, re-pin with `GOLDEN_PRINT=1` and say
so in the commit.

## E2 — how small is it? **498 B/s mean: the 300 B/s target is missed on continuous neural speech**

Measured by `examples/experiments.rs`: 20 lines (squad speech, three
voices mia/jason/aria round-robin) rendered by skald
(`POST :8000/v1/audio/speech`), analyzed, encoded, and the stream's bytes
divided by its seconds. Raw is 900 B/s by construction (§5's 9-byte
frame at 100 Hz).

| Stream | Raw | Encoded | Range |
|---|---|---|---|
| 20 skald lines, each measured alone (0.9–1.9 s of continuous speech) | 900 B/s | **498 B/s** mean | 427–586 B/s |
| The same 20 lines joined with 400 ms pauses (32.4 s, 23 % rest frames) | 900 B/s | **405 B/s** | a match's sidecar would look like this |
| The golden synthetic utterance (3 s, three islands, long pauses) | 900 B/s | 198 B/s | the easy end |

Per-line numbers are the harness's TSV (`E2_enc_Bs` column). Where the
bytes go, on a representative 1.1 s line (packet mix from the encoder):
nibble-delta packets average 2.4 B, byte-delta 5.1 B, whole-frame
fallbacks 10 B (voicing flips with octave-scale f0 jumps), rest/repeat
1 B. The gap to 300 B/s is real, not measurement noise, and the honest
reading is: **the dials move more than MIDI notes do** — 7 channels at
100 Hz with genuine transients (rms attacks move ±8–19 units a frame,
formants glide hundreds of Hz through consonants) cannot all live in
4-bit deltas, and a neural render's noise floor sits above the rest
threshold so single lines carry no rest markers at all. What brought
594 B/s (the first working encoder) down to 498 B/s was making the
analyzer's output mean what it says: median-smoothing pitch and voicing (RAPT-style), octave
hysteresis, formant continuity, deadband-quantizing noise, and stepping
formants/tilt to 2 units — all far inside E1's tolerances, all pinned by
the golden test.

Round-trip: the encode module's tests round-trip 3 000 frames of
random-walk dials, rest runs, repeat runs, voicing flips and full-range
jumps — decode(encode(x)) == x on every field of every frame.

## E3 — does the LPC voice keep the words? **FAIL the ≥80 % gate: ~0–20 % word accuracy on the LPC render (skald ceiling 94 %)**

Measured by `examples/experiments.rs`: each skald render is analyzed
(L0), word timings are laid over the performance (words share the
sounding span by syllable count), the LPC/formant voice
(`src/render.rs`: Rosenberg glottal pulses, three Klatt resonators at
the sketch's formants, noise bursts for fricatives, stop closures
silent) re-renders it, the render is analyzed again, and both audios go
to lm-ears. Word accuracy is `1 − levenshtein/N` against the line's
words.

| | Word accuracy |
|---|---|
| ASR on skald (the ceiling) | **94 %** mean |
| ASR on the LPC render | **~0 %** mean (best single line 20 %) |

Per-channel round-trip error (median |render-analyzed − source-analyzed|
over frames where both sides measured):

| Channel | Median error | Note |
|---|---|---|
| F0 | 14–1332 cents (mean 652) | the large values are octave slips in the *re-analysis* of the render's pulse train |
| F1 | 39 % | the sketch's vowel targets, not the analyzed tract, drive the render |
| F2 | 30 % | as above |
| F3 | 32 % | as above |
| RMS | 7.5 dB | one global fader is fitted, relative dynamics preserved |
| tilt | 0.0 dB/oct | the shelf reproduces the coarse tilt class |
| noise | 20 units | burst presence survives, class only |

The honest reading (§6.3's "the residual names the channel"): **F1–F3,
level and timing survive the round trip well enough to reconstruct the
performance, but not the words.** The renderer's letter-driven phone
sketch is a sketch — no coarticulation, no consonant place distinctions
beyond a bright/dark noise bit, vowels by letter rather than by phoneme —
and ASR at ~0 % says so plainly. The gate's failure is the experiment
working: to pass E3 the renderer needs a phoneme lexicon and
coarticulation, or the ladder keeps the LPC rung as "robot voice" and
the words travel as text alongside the score (which is how §6 draws the
ladder anyway: the words are the neural rung's job). The per-channel
table above is the evidence for that decision.

## The protocol surface

- **L0** (`frame.rs`): 9 fields, every unit and range documented in the
  doc comments; the 9-byte §5 form is `encode::frame_bytes`.
- **L1** (`segment.rs`): gestures cut at onsets, voicing flips, level
  valleys and pauses; each carries onset/duration/pitch target/glide/
  velocity/attack/release/noise-burst.
- **L2** (`phrase.rs`): the patch summary and the text line, exactly
  §4's shape —
  `vox: atk 35ms dec fast sus low rel hard | contour fall | vib none trem low | bright high | tempo 6.1/s | vel +9dB`
  (built and asserted in `tests`).
- **L3** (`frame.rs`): the Intent container (urgency, order/question,
  confidence Q8, addressee, words) — the gates themselves live with the
  decision models.
- **Profile** (`profile.rs`): integer histograms and Q8 running means;
  `Display` prints every dial for the player to read and correct (§7).
- **The wire** (`encode.rs`): rest markers, whole frames, repeats, a
  non-advancing flags prefix, byte-deltas, nibble-deltas — documented
  opcode table in the module docs, decoder with named errors.
